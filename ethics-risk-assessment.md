# Ethics and Risk Assessment

**Study:** Usability research for [PRODUCT]  
**Researcher:** Krish Patel  
**Status:** Draft — approval required before participant-facing activity

## Ethical principles

The study will apply informed consent, voluntary participation, respect for participant autonomy, fair recruitment, data minimisation, confidentiality safeguards and accurate reporting.

## Risk register

| Risk | Potential impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Participant feels pressured | Consent may not be freely given | State clearly that participation is voluntary and withdrawal has no penalty |
| Participant discomfort | Distress or reluctance to continue | Allow skipped questions, breaks and stopping |
| Unnecessary personal data collected | Privacy exposure | Collect only information needed for the approved research question |
| Sensitive information appears during a task | Accidental disclosure | Stop or redirect the task; do not copy or retain the information |
| Unapproved recording | Privacy and consent breach | Do not record unless approved and separately consented to |
| Research notes identify a participant | Re-identification risk | Use participant codes and remove direct identifiers |
| Unauthorised access | Disclosure of research data | Use approved storage and access controls |
| Leading questions or moderator bias | Distorted findings | Use neutral wording and a consistent task script |
| Findings overstated | Incorrect product decisions | Separate observation from interpretation and state limitations |
| Data retained longer than authorised | Increased exposure | Follow the programme's approved retention and deletion schedule |

## Data that will not be intentionally collected

- Passwords, authentication tokens or security answers.
- Bank, payment or card details.
- Government identification numbers.
- Precise home addresses.
- Private messages or unrelated personal content.
- Medical or health information.
- Sensitive personal attributes not required by the approved research.
- Unnecessary photographs, audio or video.

## Bias and quality controls

- Use consistent task wording across sessions.
- Avoid suggesting the expected answer.
- Do not coach participants through tasks unless necessary.
- Record what happened before interpreting why it happened.
- Keep contradictory observations.
- Do not treat a small exploratory sample as statistically representative.
