# Research Plan

**Checkpoint:** LAB-90ABC508 — Week 1: Research question and ethics  
**Researcher:** Krish Patel  
**Working title:** Understanding usability and task completion in [PRODUCT]  
**Status:** Draft — requires project-specific confirmation and approval

## 1. Background

This study will examine how intended users understand and interact with [PRODUCT] while attempting a small set of representative tasks. It will identify points of confusion, friction, error or unmet expectation. The study will use participant evidence rather than relying solely on design assumptions.

## 2. Decision this study will support

The study is intended to support a product decision about **which usability issue or workflow should be prioritised for the next iteration of [PRODUCT]**.

The study will not independently determine business strategy, product-market fit or population-wide user preferences.

## 3. Primary research question

How effectively can intended users complete the selected core tasks in [PRODUCT], and what usability barriers affect their ability to do so?

## 4. Secondary research questions

- Can participants identify where to begin each selected task?
- Which labels, instructions or interface elements are unclear?
- Where do participants hesitate, make errors or request help?
- Does the product communicate the result of a user action clearly?
- What do participants expect to happen at key points in the workflow?
- Which observed issues appear most consequential to task completion?

## 5. Proposed method

Use moderated exploratory usability sessions, followed by a short semi-structured interview.

During a session, participants will attempt the same approved tasks. The researcher will observe behaviour, note task outcomes and ask neutral follow-up questions. The study is qualitative and exploratory; a small sample must not be presented as statistically representative.

## 6. Proposed participants and boundary

- Proposed participants: 5–8 adults aged 18 or older, subject to the approved research plan.
- Recruitment: voluntary and based on the intended user profile for [PRODUCT].
- Session duration: approximately 20–30 minutes.
- Participation: one session per participant.
- Exclude anyone who does not meet the approved eligibility criteria or does not provide informed consent.

The exact user profile and recruitment criteria must be confirmed from the project brief before recruitment.

## 7. Draft session flow

1. Welcome the participant and explain the study.
2. Provide participant information and the consent script.
3. Confirm consent before beginning.
4. Read each approved task using the same neutral wording.
5. Observe without coaching unless intervention is necessary.
6. Ask brief, neutral follow-up questions.
7. Explain how session information will be handled.
8. Thank the participant and provide a contact route for questions or withdrawal requests, as specified by the approved protocol.

## 8. Analysis approach

- Use participant IDs such as P01 and P02 in working notes.
- Record observable behaviour separately from interpretation.
- Group similar observations into themes.
- Track task completion, errors, assistance and notable hesitation where these can be observed consistently.
- Preserve contradictory evidence.
- Prioritise issues by their observed impact on selected tasks, not by researcher preference alone.

## 9. Limitations

The proposed small exploratory sample can identify usability problems and generate hypotheses, but it cannot establish population-level prevalence. Findings may also be affected by participant familiarity, test environment, selected tasks and moderator influence.

## 10. Approval gate

No participant recruitment, interviews, testing, recording or personal-data collection should begin until the research scope, consent wording, data handling and any required recording arrangements have received the required CelesticLabs approval.
